F.A.E. MANAGEMENT · THE OPERATING METHOD

The F.A.E. Way.

Every role at F.A.E. — coach, court attendant, admin, marketer, custodian, referee — is trained and deployed the same way. This page is that method. Read it once. Every role playbook is built on top of it.

The idea is simple: nobody is left to guess. We tell you the exact standard, we train you to it, and we check that you can do it — before you ever work a shift alone.

01

Why we do it this way

Most jobs here start with "watch for a day, then you're on your own." That is how mistakes happen and how good people get blamed for things nobody ever taught them. We have all seen the two habits that hurt a team:

HABIT 1
Waiting to be told

Standing around until someone gives an order. At F.A.E. that is not respect — it is lost time. Every role has a "when you're free, do this" list so you always know the next move without being told.

HABIT 2
The quiet "yes"

Nodding that you understand when you don't, because asking feels like admitting weakness. Here, the opposite is true. We would rather you ask twice than guess once. That is why training ends with you showing us, not telling us.

The promise this method makes to you

If you were trained the F.A.E. Way and something still goes wrong, that is on the training, not on you. That only holds if the training was real — which is what the rest of this page guarantees.

02

The one rule of training

Nobody works a shift alone until their sign-off sheet is signed.

Not "probably ready," not "seems fine." A named trainer watched you do the job, you showed them you can handle the hard parts, and you both signed and dated the sheet. Until then you are always working beside someone. This protects the trainee, the trainer, and the business.

03

The 4 steps to working solo

Every role climbs the same four steps, in order. You do not skip a step, and the trainer decides when you are ready to move up — not the calendar.

Read & watch

Read your role playbook top to bottom. Then watch a senior person do the real job for at least one full shift or session. Ask every question that comes to mind — that is the point of this step.

Do-with-me

Now you do the job, with the trainer standing beside you. They let you lead and only step in when needed. This is where the playbook turns into muscle memory.

Teach-back

You show the trainer how you would handle the tricky moments — a no-show, an injury, a short cash count, an angry parent. Not "do you understand?" but "show me." See the next section.

Sign-off → solo

Trainer and trainee both sign the dated competency sheet. From that moment you can work that job alone. The signed sheet goes to the Head Ops Manager and is kept on file.

04

Teach-back — the "show me" step

This is the heart of the F.A.E. Way and the part most training skips. Understanding is proven by doing, not by saying yes. The trainer picks the hard scenarios for the role and asks the trainee to walk through them out loud or act them out. If the trainee freezes or guesses, that is not a failure — it just means step 2 repeats until it is solid.

stops play, gives first aid, tells the Head Ops immediately, logs it.

the cash at end of shift is ₱200 short.

logs it honestly, does not adjust the number, reports before leaving.

Why this beats a written quiz

A quiz tests memory. Teach-back tests the actual moment. Someone can tick the right box on paper and still freeze at the desk. We want to see the freeze in training, not on a live shift.

06

When you're free, do this

The single biggest change we want: idle time is work time. If your main task is done and no one has given you an order, you do not wait — you go to your role's standing list. Every playbook has one. The general version, for anyone, anytime:

Anyone, any role, when there is nothing urgent
  • Tidy your station and the areas around it — court, desk, storeroom, entrance.
  • Check the group chats you are responsible for and answer what you can.
  • Look at what the next shift or session will need and set it up early.
  • Restock what is low — drinks, forms, water, equipment.
  • Ask the person above you: "I'm free — what would help most right now?"

Asking "what would help most?" is not a sign you have no initiative. It is exactly the initiative we want.
