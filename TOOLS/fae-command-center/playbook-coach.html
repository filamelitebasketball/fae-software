FILAMELITE BASKETBALL · VOLLEYBALL · COACH PLAYBOOK

The coach on
the court.

This is the coaching job at F.A.E., from your first day as an Entry coach to running the floor as a Senior. It covers batch sessions, 1:1s, and open runs for basketball and volleyball. Read it top to bottom once, then keep the Quick Card in your bag.

You are trained and signed off the F.A.E. Way — by the Senior Coach of your sport. You never run a session type alone until you are signed for it.

01

The one rule

The session is the product. First on the court, last to leave — and every player gets seen.

Parents are paying for their child to be coached, not watched. A F.A.E. coach arrives before the players, has the court ready, and makes sure no one — not the shy kid, not the one at the back — goes home without real attention. If you remember one line, it is this one.

02

The coach ladder

Coaching is a progression, not a title you're handed. You climb by proving you can run more on your own. The Senior Coach decides when you move up — based on what you can actually do, not how long you've been here. There is room above Senior for lead and specialist roles as the team grows.

TIER 1
Entry Coach

New to the F.A.E. floor. You learn by running drills under a signed coach who stays with you.

Runs solo: nothing yet — you always coach beside someone.
TIER 2
Assistant Coach

Signed off to run the lighter sessions alone and to lead drills inside a batch under the Senior's plan.

Runs solo: open runs, and 1:1s once signed for them.
TIER 3
Senior Coach

Owns the batch program. First on court, last to leave. Trains and signs off Entry & Assistant coaches, and referees.

Runs solo: any session — batch, 1:1, open run, tournament.
How you move up: the open-run gate

An Entry coach becomes an Assistant the day a Senior signs that they can run an open run on their own — court prepared, players managed, safety handled, list and payments reported. That first solo open run is the proof. From there you earn each higher session type the same way: trained, comfortable, signed.

Never leave players unsupervised on the court — not for a phone call, not to take a payment. If you must step away and no other coach is there, the session pauses. Never run a batch program you were not given; if you don't have the Senior's plan, ask before the session, not during it.

04

The session, start to finish

Same spine for every session type. The batch has the most steps; a 1:1 or open run is a lighter version of the same flow.

BeforeARRIVE EARLY
  1. Arrive before the players. Court ready: floor clear, lights on, nets/rings checked.
  2. Jerseys latag (laid out) before start if the session uses them.
  3. Speaker, mic, powerbank ready and connected if needed.
  4. Have the session program the Senior gave you. No plan = ask now.
  5. Check the attendance list — who's expected today.
DuringCOACH, DON'T WATCH
  1. Greet every parent and player as they arrive — batiin lagi.
  2. Run the program. Every player gets real coaching time.
  3. Mark who attended; note anyone absent without advice.
  4. Balance coaching with capturing — a few good photos/clips, opening and closing story. Coaching comes first.
  5. Watch safety the whole time — hydration, rough play, hazards.
AfterCLOSE CLEAN
  1. Cool down, gather the group, one message to end on.
  2. Pack down: jerseys collected, equipment away, court left clean.
  3. Send the capture to whoever posts (marketer / admin).
  4. Thank parents & players in the GC, send next session's reminder.
  5. Report attendance and your session accomplishment.
Low turnout — do you still run it?

If very few players list or show up, do not decide alone to cancel or hand the slot to a renter. Ask the Senior Coach or Head Ops first — that slot may be promised. Kulitin the GC to list early so the call is made in advance, not at the door.

06

When you're free, do this

Coach standing list — between drills, between sessions, do these without being told
  • Reset the court and equipment for the next drill or the next session.
  • Help capture — the other coach's session still needs an opening/closing story.
  • Update the open-run list (teens, kids, adults, volleyball); chase the GC to list early.
  • Follow up players who haven't confirmed attendance or sizes.
  • Ask the Senior or Head Ops: "I'm free — what would help most right now?"

07

Teach-back — show your Senior before you go solo

Your Senior will have you show, not tell, how you handle these before signing you off. Freezing here just means more do-with-me — it is not a mark against you.

a player twists an ankle mid-drill.

stop play, first aid, tell Head Ops now, log it, call parent/clinic if needed.

a parent says their child isn't getting enough attention.

listen, stay calm, commit to a fix that session, tell the Senior — never argue in front of others.

only 3 players show for a batch.

does not cancel or give the slot away alone — asks Senior/Head Ops first.

the court is wet / a ring is loose before start.

fix or make safe first; if it can't be made safe, pause and escalate before any player is on it.

you're running an open run alone and a fight nearly breaks out.

step in early, separate, calm it, log it; call security/Head Ops if it escalates.

08

When to escalate

NOW

Any injury, a fight, anyone threatening a player, coach or parent, a court hazard you can't make safe.

TODAY

A serious parent complaint, a player sent home upset, damaged equipment, a session you couldn't run.

THIS WEEK

Repeated low turnout, a player dropping off, equipment wearing out, a scheduling clash you keep hitting.

Quick Contacts — Head Ops, Co-Owner, Maintenance, Security, Clinic — are on the Command Center home page, tap to call.
